The original chart compares GDP per capita and life expectancy across countries. GDP per capita is placed on a logarithmic horizontal axis, life expectancy on the vertical axis, continent is encoded by color, and population by bubble size.

The redesign below uses a historical Gapminder-style country dataset (1952–2007) with country, continent, year, life expectancy, population, and GDP per capita. It is an equivalent external dataset used to implement the redesign in D3.js.

2. Intended Message, Audience, and Tasks

Intended message

Countries with higher GDP per capita generally have higher life expectancy, but the relationship is nonlinear and varies across countries and regions. Viewers should also notice regional clustering and outliers.

Audience

The primary audience is a broad public interested in global development, economics, and population health, including both casual readers and users who want to inspect particular countries.

Tasks

  • Identify the overall GDP–life expectancy relationship
  • Compare countries and regions
  • Locate specific countries
  • Find outliers
  • Inspect exact values
  • Explore change over time

3. Critique of the Original

Strength 1 — Effective quantitative channels

The two primary quantitative variables use horizontal and vertical position, which supports accurate comparison and makes the overall relationship visible quickly.

Strength 2 — Compact multivariate context

Color adds continent and bubble area adds population without requiring separate views. The logarithmic GDP scale is also appropriate for a variable spanning a very wide range.

Weakness 1 — Bubble area and overlap

Problem: population is encoded through area, so large bubbles can cover nearby smaller countries.

Why it matters: area is less precise than position and the large bubbles compete visually with the main GDP–life expectancy relationship.

Improvement: use nearly uniform points and move population to details-on-demand.

Weakness 2 — Label clutter without complete lookup

Problem: selected permanent labels occupy space, while many countries remain unlabeled.

Why it matters: the view becomes cluttered but still does not support systematic country lookup.

Improvement: replace most labels with search, hover, and persistent selection.

Weakness 3 — Logarithmic scale can be overlooked

Problem: the logarithmic GDP scale is appropriate but easy for casual users to miss.

Why it matters: equal horizontal distances do not represent equal absolute GDP differences.

Improvement: retain the log scale but label it explicitly and use interpretable currency ticks.

Weakness 4 — Exact comparison is cumbersome

Problem: comparing two countries requires repeatedly reading both axes and moving attention across the chart.

Why it matters: this increases effort for inspect and compare tasks.

Improvement: provide a persistent selected-country detail panel.

5. Major Redesign Decisions

Decision 1 — Reduce bubble-size dominance

Changed: points use a nearly uniform radius instead of strongly encoding population by area. Why: population is secondary to the relationship between GDP and life expectancy. Problem addressed: overlap and difficult area comparison. Improvement: the primary x/y relationship receives stronger visual priority, while population remains available on demand.

Decision 2 — Replace permanent labels with search, hover, and selection

Changed: most labels are removed and country lookup is handled through interaction. Why: labels create clutter in dense areas. Problem addressed: visual congestion and incomplete identification. Improvement: search supports locate, hover supports identify, and click supports persistent inspection.

Decision 3 — Make the logarithmic scale explicit

Changed: the log scale is retained but clearly named, and currency ticks are formatted as $1k, $2k, $5k, etc. Why: the transformation is useful but can be overlooked. Problem addressed: scale misinterpretation. Improvement: viewers receive the benefits of the log scale with clearer guidance.

Decision 4 — Add regional focus and a persistent detail panel

Changed: continent filtering de-emphasizes other regions rather than removing them, and selected-country values stay visible in a side panel. Why: users need both context and precise inspection. Problem addressed: difficult regional and exact-value comparison. Improvement: users can focus on a region while preserving global context and can inspect exact country values without repeatedly reading the axes.

6. Original vs. Redesign

Easier after redesign

  • Locate any country
  • Retrieve exact values
  • Focus on one continent
  • Compare years
  • Understand the log axis

Trade-offs

Population differences are less immediately visible because population is no longer encoded by large bubble areas. The redesign also requires interaction for some details that were visible immediately in the original.

Limitations

The scatterplot shows association, not causation. In addition, the redesign uses an equivalent historical dataset rather than the exact current OWID dataset, so the data years and source lineage differ from the original chart.
