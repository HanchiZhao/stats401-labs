Design Description

Both treemaps use rectangle area to encode GDP because area provides a compact way to compare the relative economic size of countries within their areas and continents. GDP status is encoded using color: green indicates an increase, gray indicates no change, and red indicates a decrease. Country labels are displayed when enough space is available, while tooltips provide detailed information without overcrowding small rectangles.

The squarified treemap creates rectangles with relatively balanced aspect ratios, making countries easier to compare visually. The slice-dice layout emphasizes the hierarchical subdivision by alternating the direction of partitioning across levels. This makes the structure easier to follow, but can create narrow rectangles that are harder to compare precisely.
